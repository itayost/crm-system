CRM · Client side Proposal · 2026-08-17 Successor to “Ledger Console”

The client side stops being a form with a status list.

It becomes the place a client gets answered — in one sentence, on a phone, in about forty seconds. The console is a ledger you operate. The portal is a letter you receive, and it should be made of different material.

The user model

One person, one phone, one question.

A client of this CRM is a small-business owner. They do not log in — there is no password, because the URL is the credential. They get a WhatsApp message, tap a link, and arrive holding exactly one of three questions:

  • Is anything waiting on me? — almost always a quote to approve.
  • What is happening with my thing? — status, and when.
  • I need to tell them something. — a bug, a request, a file.

They will be on the page for well under a minute, one-handed, probably outdoors. Then they go back to WhatsApp, where the actual relationship lives. The portal exists for the two things WhatsApp is genuinely bad at: seeing state and answering a formal question. Everything else on the surface is a distraction from those two jobs.

Design consequences
  • The answer comes before the evidence, always. A client should not have to assemble a conclusion out of counters.
  • 17px body, not 13px. This is read in sunlight by someone who is not paid to look at it.
  • 48px tap targets, one-handed reach, the decision pinned above the fold.
  • Never promise a channel that is switched off — the bot can be paused, and the copy already knows it.
  • No dark mode. The portal ships light only, deliberately, and these mockups render light on either theme for that reason.
Where it is today

The portal is the best-built surface in the repo, pointed at the wrong reader.

Server components throughout, every number bidi-isolated, real links, the only mobile-first code in the project. What it is not is a client's surface: it is the operator console's design system, at the operator's density, answering the operator's questions.

The counters

Three figures — requests, in development, to pay — that all read 0 on a good day. The console has a rule that a badge is only allowed on a number that can reach zero on a good day. Here it inverts: a healthy client opens their portal and is shown three zeros and a form.

The form

Six fields and a file input are the main event of the home page, above anything about their actual work. The file control renders No file chosen — English, in the middle of a Hebrew RTL page, in the one form a paying customer ever fills in.

The dates

Nothing is dated and nothing points forward. A request shows a status chip and no history, so “is this moving?” — the actual question — has no answer on the page. Every timestamp needed for one is already a column.

The promise

A phase in PENDING_APPROVAL renders to the client as ממתין לאישורך — “waiting for your approval” — and there is no approval control anywhere in the portal. The surface asks for something it cannot accept.

The intake

The WhatsApp agent interviews the client — where, what happened, what they expected, how often, is it blocking — and stores the answers. They are folded into a task description for the owner and shown to the client, who supplied them, never.

The direction

Paper and ink, and colour that only ever means a state.

The console is cold grey, hairlines and 32px rows, because an operator scans it all day. The portal gets the opposite material: a warm ground, an editorial Hebrew serif, and enough air that one thing per screen is obvious. This page is drawn in the console's material so the difference is visible before it is argued.

There is no decorative brand hue, on purpose. --primary becomes ink rather than blue — the rebuild doc flagged that --primary, --link and --tone-info-solid are all the same blue doing three unrelated jobs, and proposed exactly this; it never shipped, and a portal-scoped override lands it without touching the console. All eight status tones keep their existing meanings; only the eight --tone-*-surface washes are re-mixed warm so they sit on cream instead of grey. Foreground, mark and solid are untouched, so contrast is unchanged and the tone tests keep passing.

The rule a client can actually learn from one visit: on this surface, colour only ever means a state. They have no legend and no training, so every hue they see has to be worth something.

Density and material

Shadow on a card is the one place this deliberately breaks the console's rule that structure comes from hairlines. A dense ledger is flat because everything on it is equally available; a portal has one thing that matters per screen, and lifting it off the paper is how a phone says so.

The signature device

The journey rail.

The console's signature is the phase strip: width is price, fill is work state, an ink underline is paid. Exactly right for an operator, and exactly wrong for a client — it says “this step matters more because it costs more”, and at 10px on a phone it cannot carry a date. The client's version is vertical, dated, and shows the future.

  1. אפיון ותוכן 18/06 · 2,400 ₪
  2. עיצוב מסכים 09/07 · 3,200 ₪
  3. פיתוח החזית 04/08 · 3,600 ₪ · הושלם
  4. חיבור מערכת הזמנות בעבודה 3,800 ₪
  5. העלאה לאוויר 1,800 ₪
Behind you — filled ink, dated. A step that happened gets a solid marker and the date it happened on. That is the whole reassurance mechanism: proof, with a timestamp, that the thing moved.
Now — the only tone-coloured step on the rail. Exactly one marker carries hue. Colour is scarce here precisely so that this one is unmissable at arm's length.
Ahead — a hollow ring, a dotted connector, no date. The shape of what is left, rendered as outline. This is the half that answers “what happens next” without the client having to ask, and it is the half the portal has never had.
Paid stays an ink underline. Inherited from the phase strip and non-negotiable. Work state owns the colour, money owns the mark. Rendering “paid” in green collapses the two axes the device exists to keep apart — approval and payment are separate facts, and the schema is emphatic about it.
One device, two uses. The phases of a project, and the events of a request. A client learns it once.

An honest limit, encoded rather than papered over: Request has no startedAt column, so “work started” genuinely cannot be dated. The current step therefore renders as the marked, undated now — not an invented timestamp. The existing owner-side timeline already states this constraint about itself; the client version inherits it.

Implementation

How it gets built.

Nothing here needs a migration, a new API, or a new column. Every field the redesign shows is already in the database and already reaches the server — it is simply never rendered.

Routes

/r/[token] home rewritten /r/[token]/requests list rewritten /r/[token]/requests/new submit flow NEW static child — no clash with [requestId] /r/[token]/[requestId] request + decision rewritten URL unchanged: live WhatsApp deep links keep landing /r/[token]/projects list rewritten /r/[token]/projects/[projectId] project detail NEW /r/[token]/loading.tsx skeleton NEW /r/[token]/error.tsx recoverable failure NEW

The decision deliberately does not get its own route. Quote notices already deep-link to /r/{token}/{requestId}; making that page render decision-first when a quote is outstanding puts the answer above the fold without an extra tap and without breaking a link already sitting in a client's WhatsApp history.

Tokens — one scoped block, everything re-skins

app/globals.css [data-surface='portal'] { … } applied by app/r/[token]/layout.tsx re-points the existing semantic layer: --surface-app --card --foreground-* --border --primary --radius-* plus the eight --tone-*-surface washes, warmed for cream so StatusPill / Input / Label / Textarea / Select re-skin with no new utilities and no new mechanism tailwind.config.js a small portal colour group for genuinely new concepts (paper-raised, paper-sunken, rail geometry) app/layout.tsx Frank Ruhl Libre as --font-display, preload:false so it never enters the console's critical path

client-view.ts — the one module that decides what a client may see

This module is read by the portal and by the WhatsApp agent's getMyRequests. Every new field goes through it, or the two surfaces start telling one client two different stories about the same request.

  • Add to the select: clientDecisionNote — the client writes up to 1,000 characters explaining a decline and can never read it back — and intake.
  • Whitelist intake field by field. Parse with the existing readIntake(), expose the seven answers the client gave, and drop suggestedType — it is a machine guess documented as a hint for the owner only. Never spread the raw JSON.
  • Attachments gain a descriptor — { index, name, kind } derived server-side. Storage paths still never leave the module, and files are still addressed by index, because the bucket is shared with WhatsApp support media.
  • New buildClientTimeline(). It cannot reuse the owner's: that one is written from the owner's chair (הלקוח אישר, נוצרה משימה) and reads fields outside the whitelist. The client version is second person, drops internal events, and keeps the ghosted-future idea.
  • Move notYetDue out of the component and into the module. It is computed in JSX today, which is precisely how the bot's answer and the portal's drift apart.

Writes

  • One new Server Action, attachToRequest(token, requestId, formData) — token-scoped and rate-limited like its two neighbours, reusing the existing storage validation, and notifying the owner.
  • The public submit route moves from form.get('file') to form.getAll('file'), capped at five.
  • No JSON GET API is added. The portal's read model stays “server components hit Prisma directly”, which is the reason there is nothing to enumerate and no CORS surface.

Sequence

  1. Material. Token scope, the serif, the shell, and the three missing states — loading, error, invalid token.
  2. The data. client-view.ts: intake, decline note, attachment descriptors, the client timeline, notYetDue.
  3. The rail. The journey rail component, over both phases and events.
  4. The decision. The awaiting mode of the request route — the highest-value screen on the surface.
  5. The request. Intake playback, named attachments, the upload action.
  6. The rest. Home, the grouped list, project detail.
  7. Intake. The submit flow and the multi-file public route.
Out of scope, deliberately
  • Phase approval from the portal. It would move money — APPROVED stamps approvedAt, which is what “invoices worth chasing” counts. Worth building, and worth deciding on its own rather than smuggling into a redesign. Until then the label stops asking for something the page cannot accept.
  • Payment status per invoice, and a pay link. New data the schema does not have, and a payment provider decision.
  • An in-portal comment thread. Two code comments already reject this on purpose: the conversation lives in WhatsApp, and a thread nobody opens would be a worse version of it.
